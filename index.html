Introduction

For most of the last decade I have been doing one thing in a lot of different forms: taking work that is hard to explain and making it clear enough that people care about it.

That has meant a state wildlife agency explaining disease testing to hunters, a university marketing office, ongoing marketing for client businesses, and narrative short films I wrote and directed.

Architects have a phrase for what a building owes you: commodity, firmness and delight. Most business communication manages the first, sometimes the second, and rarely thinks about the third.

Education

I hold a B.A. in Media and Journalism from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, where I specialized in Public Relations. I still work the way that training taught: figure out what needs explaining before figuring out how to shoot it.

Commercial work

Private clients, where the work has to keep running month after month and somebody has to own whether it does.

Being the whole marketing department

Retained single-point marketing partner for two luxury real estate teams, as their sole outside marketer. Strategy, production, web, email and social, on a monthly cadence, with one person carrying the context forward instead of a rotating cast of vendors.

Marketing materials for dozens of multi-million-dollar listings across those clients, including print advertising produced for a paid placement in the Wall Street Journal.

Making work that compounds

For one top-producing luxury team, 100 or more social and vlog-style videos over a multi-year retainer, generating hundreds of thousands of organic views across Instagram, Facebook and Nextdoor. Not one campaign. A body of work that kept building on itself.

Websites and email programs

A WordPress to IDX migration with custom listing pages, and an email program run in Mailchimp to a list of more than 2,000 subscribers.

Behind the camera

The part I do myself. Architectural and commercial exteriors, resort and destination work across the southeastern US, and aerial capture under an FAA Part 107 certificate. All of it shot on site.

Freelance camera operator on fishing segments that aired on the Outdoor Channel, shot on early-morning field calls. Different discipline from client work: no second take, and the light does not wait.
